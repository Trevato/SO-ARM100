Safe zone

The server runs every target through the arm's own geometry: the URDF and its meshes. A pose that puts any part below the table, or into the base, is a violation. enforce refuses such moves and holds a stream at the boundary until the leader backs off. warn only reports them, in the banner and the metrics. off skips the check.

Trim the model onto the arm

The model takes the middle of each calibrated range as zero, the LeRobot convention. The real zero can be a few degrees off, which puts the model a centimetre or two away from the arm at the gripper. Compare the 3D view with the camera and adjust a trim until they match. The zone uses the trimmed model.

3D calibration: teach the arm where the camera is

The camera sees a marker. The arm needs that as a position in its own space. Two calibrations give it that.

  1. Camera. The lens geometry: focal length and distortion. Found from 15 views of the printed ChArUco board: a checkerboard with a small marker in each white square. Download it below and print it at 100 %. Output: camera.json.
  2. Hand-eye. The arm holds a marker in 6 to 8 poses. The camera says where the marker is. The arm's model says where the gripper is. The solver finds the camera's position relative to the arm's base. Output: handeye.json.

After both, a cube the camera sees becomes a target the arm can reach. With two cameras, each one gets its own pair of calibrations (camera2, handeye2); two views then give a full orientation.

Print these

Three sheets, at 100 % scale on matte paper. Check with a ruler: a cube marker's black square is 40 mm, a board square 30 mm. Tape the six cube markers on a 40 to 50 mm block, and the gripper marker flat on the outside of the fixed jaw.

About

      
This page drives an SO-101, the open robot arm by The Robot Studio, with the calibration conventions of Hugging Face LeRobot. The 3D view uses the arm's own URDF through urdf-loader and three.js. The code, the build notes and the quest are in the repository's Software/ folder.
